## Why this task exists

ROADMAP #9, and the wave gate says it plainly: **only open this if the in-app "Heads up"
card has proven insufficient in real use.** The decision on record was to prefer in-app
reminders over notifications. Push on iOS is constrained — it needs the PWA actually
installed to the home screen plus an explicit permission grant, and the app is keyless and
served privately over Tailscale from the user's own Mac mini, which is an unusual shape for
a push setup. Before anyone writes a service-worker push handler, someone should establish
whether it works at all on this deployment.

## Objective

Determine whether a thaw/prep reminder can be delivered as a real iOS notification from
*this* deployment — installed PWA, Tailscale-private origin, `meal-server.py` on the Mac
mini, no third-party service and no API key — and what it would cost. Produce a
recommendation: build, build differently, or close #9 for good.

## In scope

- What iOS actually requires today for web push to an installed PWA (permission model,
  installation requirement, HTTPS/origin constraints).
- Whether the Tailscale HTTPS origin and `meal-server.py` can satisfy those constraints,
  including whether a push subscription survives on a private tailnet origin.
- What a push server would mean for the keyless principle — VAPID keys are keys.
- The realistic failure mode: a reminder that must fire *while the phone is asleep and off
  the tailnet* is the actual use case, and it's the one most likely not to work.
- A concrete alternative if push fails: e.g. a scheduled Claude-side nudge, or making the
  existing in-app "Heads up" harder to miss.

## Out of scope

- **Building it.** If the answer is "yes, feasible", add a follow-on brief and
  row in `program/TASKS.md`; don't start it in this session.
- Any hosted or third-party push service. That reopens the keyless decision, which is
  settled (README §Anti-patterns) and is the user's call, not this task's.
- Changing the in-app "Heads up" behavior. You may *recommend* changes.

## Known traps for THIS task

- **Documentation is one comparator.** iOS web-push behavior has changed repeatedly and is
  widely misreported; a claim needs a real check on a real installed PWA, or it's marked
  INCONCLUSIVE (CONTRACT §4). Don't launder a blog post into a verdict.
- A notification test that only ever fires with the app open proves nothing about the case
  that matters.
- "Keyless & private" is a founding principle, not a preference. Any design that ships a
  key into the app is dead on arrival regardless of how well push works.
- Scope creep into #8 (two-way sync) — a push channel is not a sync channel.
